# Soul Match
## A fictional product concept

**Meet your soulmate. Preserve your presence.**

Name: ____________________  
Course: ____________________  
Date: ____________________

**Speaker notes:**
Soul Match is a speculative product, not a real service. It combines a smart ring, an app, a personalized avatar, soulmate discovery, and a digital memory feature.

---

# 1. The Principal Idea

Soul Match is a ring and app that learn about you over time.

- Apply through the app
- Receive the ring free for one month
- Pay for the service after the trial
- Let the ring observe your behavior
- Receive a personalized digital avatar

**Speaker notes:**
The central idea is that the ring learns from everyday behavior instead of asking users to complete a long profile. After the first month, the system creates an avatar that represents the user's habits and personality.

---

# 2. What Does Soul Match Do?

The avatar has two main purposes:

1. Help discover a possible soulmate
2. Preserve a digital copy of the user's personality for loved ones

The product connects relationships, identity, and memory in one experience.

**Speaker notes:**
The first purpose is romantic: the avatar helps identify a person who might be a good match. The second purpose is emotional and futuristic: a copy of the user could remain available for a family member or child after the user's death.

---

# 3. The User Journey

```text
Apply in the app
        ↓
Receive the Soul Match ring
        ↓
One month of behavior scanning
        ↓
Personal avatar is created
        ↓
Avatar suggests a soulmate
        ↓
Digital copy is saved for loved ones
```

**Speaker notes:**
This is the complete journey from sign-up to the long-term memory feature. The free first month gives users time to experience the ring before the paid subscription begins.

---

# 4. Empathy

### Possible users

- Someone curious about finding a partner
- Someone who struggles to describe their personality
- A parent thinking about their child's future
- A family thinking about memory and legacy

### Emotional questions

- What would users want technology to understand about them?
- Would a digital copy feel comforting or strange?
- What would loved ones need during grief?

**Speaker notes:**
Empathy means considering the people and emotions behind the product. Soul Match is not only a dating idea. The memory feature involves grief, family, identity, and trust, so it needs especially sensitive design.

---

# 5. Defining Needs

Soul Match should be:

- Personal: it learns from the individual user
- Simple: the ring works quietly in the background
- Transparent: users understand the subscription and data use
- Private: behavior data belongs to the user
- Sensitive: the memory feature respects grief and consent

**User need statement:**

> People need a simple and personal way to understand their relationships and preserve meaningful parts of themselves, while remaining in control of their data and identity.

**Speaker notes:**
The product cannot only be interesting. It must also make users feel informed and in control. The free trial, paid subscription, behavior scanning, and memory copy all need clear explanations.

---

# 6. Generating Ideas

The idea grew by connecting several questions:

- What if a ring could learn from behavior?
- What if it could create an avatar of its owner?
- What if that avatar could help with soulmate discovery?
- What if the avatar could also preserve memories?

### Result

A wearable product that begins as a dating service and becomes a digital legacy service.

**Speaker notes:**
Instead of treating these as separate products, the concept connects them through the avatar. The avatar is the bridge between understanding who someone is, finding a compatible person, and preserving a version of that identity.

---

**Speaker notes:**
SCAMPER helped expand the original idea. The most important changes were combining the ring with an avatar, adapting behavior tracking, and putting the avatar to another use as a digital memory for loved ones.

---

# 8. Prototype and Future MVP

The full idea is speculative. The future MVP would test only the most important parts:

- App onboarding and one-month trial explanation
- Ring data visualized in the app
- Personalized avatar preview
- Example soulmate recommendation
- Consent and privacy controls
- Clear subscription and cancellation information

**Speaker notes:**
The MVP would not immediately promise to recreate a whole person. It would test whether users understand the concept and feel comfortable with the avatar and data controls. The larger memory-copy idea could be explored later through careful research.

---

# 9. Risks and Ethics

Soul Match would need to address:

- Continuous behavior monitoring
- Sensitive personal data
- Clear consent and data ownership
- Subscription transparency after one month
- Emotional dependence on a digital copy
- Whether an avatar can truly represent a person
- How the product should handle grief

**Speaker notes:**
These risks are part of the product, not an afterthought. A digital copy should never be presented as the actual person or as a replacement for a loved one. Users would need control over what is collected, what is stored, and who can access it.

---

# 10. Conclusion

Soul Match asks a big question:

> Can technology help us understand love, identity, and memory?

It begins with a ring.  
It learns through an avatar.  
It ends with a conversation about what part of a person can remain.

**Next steps:**

- Test the concept with potential users
- Design the ring and app experience
- Prototype the avatar
- Research consent and privacy
- Decide what the MVP should and should not promise

**Speaker notes:**
Soul Match is intentionally ambitious and fictional. Its value as a design concept is that it creates a conversation about relationships, personal data, identity, and memory. The next step is to reduce the idea into a responsible MVP that can be tested.
